How to record and transcribe Microsoft Teams calls on Windows

FreeCallNotes records your voice and the other people on a Teams call as two separate tracks, then transcribes them on your own PC. It's free, open source and needs no installation. This guide covers setup, the Teams settings that matter, and what to do when something doesn't sound right.

Before you start

  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC (64-bit). No GPU needed; 8 GB of RAM is recommended.
  • About 2 GB of free disk space for the app and speech model, plus about 230 MB for each hour you record.
  • An internet connection the first time only, to download the speech model (about 660 MB). After that, recording and transcription work offline. Teams itself still needs the internet, of course.
  • Headphones or a headset. Not strictly required, but they give a much cleaner transcript (see the tips below).
  • The other people's agreement. Plan how you'll tell them you're recording.

One-time setup (about five minutes)

  1. Download FreeCallNotes (a 56 MB zip file) and unzip it anywhere: Documents, Downloads or a USB stick. There's no installer and you don't need admin rights.
  2. Double-click FreeCallNotes.exe in the unzipped folder. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info and then Run anyway. This appears because the app isn't code-signed yet; only do it for a copy downloaded from the official GitHub release.
  3. Read and accept the "Before you record" notice. It's a short reminder about consent, accuracy and AI assistants.
  4. Wait for "Ready". The first time, the app downloads the speech model into its own folder. Keep the window open until it finishes. It also adds a desktop icon so you can find it next time.

Record a Teams call, step by step

  1. Open FreeCallNotes before the meeting and wait for "Ready".
  2. Join the meeting in the Teams app or in Teams on the web. Meetings, one-to-one calls and group calls all work the same way.
  3. Tell everyone you're recording and get their agreement.
  4. Press Record. Your words appear under "User" and everyone else's under "System" as they speak.
  5. Press Stop when the call ends. The finished transcript is ready a few moments later.

Set up Teams audio so both sides are recorded

FreeCallNotes doesn't connect to Teams. It records your Windows default microphone and whatever plays through your Windows default speakers or headphones. So Teams and Windows need to use the same devices:

  1. In Teams, open Settings > Devices (or Device settings from the More menu during a call) and note the speaker and microphone it uses.
  2. In Windows, open Settings > System > Sound and set the same headset as the default output and input.
  3. Make a quick test call in Teams and watch the User and System meters in FreeCallNotes move.

Using Teams in the browser? It normally plays through the Windows default device, so the same check applies.

Teams recording or FreeCallNotes?

Teams has its own recording and transcription, and if your organisation has it turned on, it's a good option. FreeCallNotes is for the calls where it isn't available or isn't what you need.

Listen-only meetings and webinars

For a town hall, training session or webinar where you only listen, open Options and untick Record the user (microphone). FreeCallNotes then records only the system sound, so coughs and keyboard noise on your side stay out of the transcript.

Where your recording is saved

Each call gets its own folder named with the date and time, inside the Call Notes folder next to the app. Click Saved calls in FreeCallNotes to open it, or pick a different location in Options under Save calls to. Inside each call folder you'll find:

  • me.wav: your microphone.
  • them.wav: everyone else, as you heard them.
  • The transcript, with timestamps and "User" and "System" labels.
  • Transcript for AI: the transcript plus instructions for an AI assistant, in one text file.

Nothing is uploaded. To delete a call, delete its folder. To remove FreeCallNotes completely, delete the app folder.

Turn the transcript into meeting notes

  1. After the call, click Show transcript for AI.
  2. Drag the file into the AI assistant you're allowed to use, or paste its contents.
  3. Send it. The instructions in the file ask for a summary, decisions, action items with owners, and open questions.

Before you share the notes, check names, numbers and anything important against the transcript or the audio. Speech recognition gets most words right but not all of them.

Getting a more accurate transcript

  • Use headphones and a decent microphone. This makes the biggest difference.
  • Turn on the second pass. In Options, set Moonshine to run After the call. It transcribes the recording a second time, which can catch words the live transcript missed.
  • Close other sound sources. The System track records everything you hear, so a video playing in another tab ends up in the transcript too.

Troubleshooting

The "System" side of the transcript is empty

FreeCallNotes records whatever plays through your Windows default speakers or headphones. If Teams sends its sound to a different device, there's nothing for it to record. In Teams, open Settings > Devices and choose the same speaker as your Windows default output, or change the Windows default to match Teams. Then check that you can hear the other people in the headphones you expect.

My own voice is missing

Check that the microphone you talk into is the Windows default microphone (Settings > System > Sound > Input). Also check Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone and make sure Let desktop apps access your microphone is on. In FreeCallNotes, open Options and make sure Record the user (microphone) is ticked.

Some lines are marked "echo from speakers"

That happens when the other people's voices come out of your speakers and your microphone hears them again. FreeCallNotes flags those lines so they don't get credited to you. Headphones stop it at the source.

The transcript lags or my PC feels slow during the call

Open Options and set NVIDIA Nemotron to After the call. FreeCallNotes then only records during the call, which uses very little of your computer, and transcribes once you press Stop.

The model download doesn't finish

The first-run download comes from Hugging Face. Some work networks and firewalls block it. Try a home network once; after that the model is stored in the app folder and no download is needed again.

The audio sounds muffled

Many Bluetooth headsets switch to a lower-quality "hands-free" mode while their microphone is in use, which affects the call itself as well as the recording. A wired or USB headset usually gives clearer audio and a better transcript.

Consent and recording at work

In many places everyone on a call has to agree before it's recorded, and the rules differ between countries and states. Tell people at the start, for example: "Is it OK if I record this call so I can take accurate notes? It stays on my computer." Keep a note of their answer.

Many companies have rules about recording Teams calls. FreeCallNotes doesn't need admin rights, but that doesn't make it approved by your employer: follow your organisation's rules on recording and on which AI assistants you may use. FreeCallNotes is a tool, not legal advice.

Questions

Does the other side know I'm recording?

No. FreeCallNotes doesn't appear in the meeting, so you need to tell people yourself and get their agreement before you record.

Do I need a Teams licence or admin permission?

No. FreeCallNotes records the audio on your own PC, so it doesn't depend on Teams recording features, your licence or admin rights.

Does it work with Teams on the web?

Yes. It records what plays through your speakers or headphones, whether Teams runs in the app or in Edge or Chrome.

Can I record a Teams meeting I'm not presenting in?

Yes, as long as you can hear it on your PC. Consent rules still apply.

Does it work with the free version of Teams?

Yes. FreeCallNotes doesn't use Teams at all, so any version works.

Can it tell the other speakers apart?

Not yet. It separates you from everyone else. The AI assistant can often work out who's who from context.

Is it free?

Yes. It's free and open source under GPL-3.0, with no account and no limits.
