A free call recorder and transcriber for Windows.

FreeCallNotes is an open-source desktop app that records your calls and turns them into text on your own PC. Use it for Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet or Webex meetings, sales and client calls, interviews, or any call you'd rather not take notes on by hand.

  • Record Zoom and Teams calls

    It captures your microphone and your computer's sound at the same time, so it records both sides of a call in any app, including calls in a browser tab, without changing your call settings.

  • Transcription without the cloud

    Speech recognition runs locally with NVIDIA Nemotron, and optionally Moonshine. Your audio is never uploaded, and after the first model download it works offline.

  • An AI notetaker without the bot

    Cloud notetakers join your meeting as an extra participant. FreeCallNotes runs quietly on your computer, and you hand the finished transcript to the AI assistant you're allowed to use.

  • A free, private alternative to cloud notetakers

    No subscription, no account and no minutes limit. The source code is public under GPL-3.0, so anyone can check what it does.

  • Portable, with no admin rights

    It's a folder, not an installer. Unzip it and run it, even on a PC where you can't install software. Follow your employer's policies on recording and AI tools.

Three steps from call to notes.

FreeCallNotes listens to your microphone and your computer's sound, the same audio you hear in your headphones. It doesn't matter which app the call happens in.

Press record

Your voice and the other side are saved as two separate tracks, and the transcript builds while you talk.

Press stop

The transcript is finished within moments and saved in a dated folder next to both audio files.

Paste into your AI

Drop the "Transcript for AI" file into your AI assistant. It includes the prompt for cleaning up the transcript and writing your notes.

What the recorder does.

It records, transcribes and gets out of the way. Writing the summary is left to the AI assistant you already use.

  • Two separate tracks

    You and the other side are recorded apart, so the transcript always knows who said what.

  • Live transcript

    Words appear while you talk, so you can check what was just said without asking.

  • Second opinion with Moonshine

    Switch on the optional Moonshine model in Options to re-transcribe the call afterwards for extra accuracy.

  • No meeting bot

    Nobody sees a notetaker join the call. It also works on phone calls routed through your PC.

  • Echo check

    If your speakers leak into your mic, the app flags the repeated lines in the transcript.

  • A file for your AI

    One file holds the transcript and a ready-written prompt, so the notes come out the same way every time.

  • Folders you control

    Each call gets a dated folder in a location you choose. Delete it whenever you like.

Privacy on your own computer.

Most AI notetakers upload your audio to be transcribed. FreeCallNotes runs the speech models locally, so after setup it works with the Wi-Fi turned off.

The code is open source on GitHub, so anyone can check exactly what it does.

Install in five steps.

There's no installer and no admin rights needed. Unzip the folder anywhere and run it. To remove it, delete the folder.

  • SystemWindows 10 or 11, 64-bit
  • Disk spaceAbout 2 GB, plus about 230 MB per hour of recording
  • InternetFirst run only
  • LanguageEnglish
  • PriceFree

  1. Download and unzip

    Get the latest release from GitHub and extract it anywhere, such as your Documents folder. Always tell people you're recording and get their consent. You're responsible for following local law.

  2. Double-click FreeCallNotes (FreeCallNotes.exe)

    If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info and then Run anyway. The warning appears because the app is new and not code-signed.

  3. Accept the "Before you record" notice

    On first run, read and accept the short Before you record notice. It covers consent, accuracy and using AI assistants, and you can reopen it from Options.

  4. Wait for "Ready to record"

    The first run downloads the speech model, about 660 MB, once. It also adds a desktop shortcut for next time.

  5. Put on headphones

    Headphones stop your mic from picking up the other side through your speakers, which keeps the transcript clean.

Questions about FreeCallNotes.

  • Is it really free?

    Yes. It's free and open source, with no account, no subscription and no limits. All the work happens on your computer. Donations may be added later, and this release stays free whatever happens.

  • Does it work on Mac or Linux?

    Not yet. It uses a Windows audio feature to capture your computer's sound. If you'd like to help bring it to other systems, the code is on GitHub.

  • Which call apps does it work with?

    Any of them. It records your default microphone and whatever plays through your default speakers or headphones, in shared mode, so your call app keeps working as normal.

  • Do I need to tell people I'm recording?

    Yes. The app doesn't announce itself, and recording laws in many countries and states require everyone on the call to agree. Ask first, and keep recordings only as long as you need them.

  • How accurate is the transcript?

    It uses NVIDIA's Nemotron streaming speech model, with an optional second pass from Moonshine. Clear English on a headset comes out well. Crosstalk and noisy rooms lower accuracy, and the AI clean-up step fixes most small slips.

  • Why doesn't it write the summary?

    Keeping the summary in your own AI assistant keeps the app small, free and private, and you choose who sees your notes.

  • Where are recordings saved?

    In a "Call Notes" folder next to the app, one dated folder per call. You can change the location in Options.

  • Does it work offline?

    Yes, after the first run. The speech model downloads once, about 660 MB, and from then on recording and transcription work without the internet.

  • Can my company use it?

    Yes, it's free for work use too. It needs no installation or admin rights, but follow your employer's policies on recording calls and using AI tools. Some company laptops block unsigned programs or downloads from Hugging Face, so check with IT if it won't start.

  • Does it tell speakers apart?

    It separates you from the other side, because they're recorded on separate tracks. It doesn't yet tell the other people on the call apart from each other.

  • Is my data used for training?

    No. Nothing leaves your computer, so there's nothing to train on.

  • Can it record a phone call?

    Only if the call's sound plays through your computer, such as a softphone, a desktop app or a phone linked to your PC. It can't hear a call on a separate handset.

  • How do I uninstall it?

    Close it and delete the folder, plus the desktop shortcut if you like. There's nothing else to remove.
