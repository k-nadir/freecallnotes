## 1. Recording calls is your responsibility

FreeCallNotes records audio: your microphone and everything your computer plays, which includes the voices of other people on a call.

- **Consent laws differ.** Some places allow you to record a call you take part in without telling anyone ("one-party consent"). Many others, including several US states, require **everyone** on the call to agree ("all-party consent"). Data-protection laws such as the UK GDPR and EU GDPR can also apply to recordings of other people, especially at work. When people on a call are in different places, the strictest rule may apply.
- **Tell people and get their agreement before you record.** The safest practice everywhere is to say at the start of the call that you are recording and why, and to stop if anyone objects.
- **Workplace rules.** Your employer, clients or regulators may have rules on recording calls, on where recordings and transcripts may be stored, how long they may be kept, and which AI tools may process them. Regulated industries (for example financial services, healthcare and legal) often have specific requirements. Check them before using FreeCallNotes at work.
- **Don't use it to record people secretly or unlawfully.** You are solely responsible for how you use FreeCallNotes and for the recordings and transcripts you make, keep or share.

The authors of FreeCallNotes do not know where or how you use it, cannot check whether a recording is lawful, and accept no responsibility for recordings made with it.

## 2. Transcripts can be wrong

Transcripts are produced automatically by speech-recognition models. They can drop words, mishear words, attribute speech to the wrong side, and get names, numbers, amounts and dates wrong. Notes written from a transcript by an AI assistant can add further mistakes.

Do not rely on a transcript, or on notes made from it, as an exact or complete record. Check anything important (figures, commitments, names, legal, medical or financial details) against the recording or with the people involved.
